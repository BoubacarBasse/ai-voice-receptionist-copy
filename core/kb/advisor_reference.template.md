# {{SHOP_NAME}} — Service Advisor Reference

How Makati uses this: recognize what the caller describes, sound experienced, stay hedged ("could be," "we sometimes see"), and land on booking an inspection. Never a certain diagnosis, never a named failed part, never a price.

## Common symptoms and what they can point to (always plural, always "possible")
- Squealing when braking — often brake wear indicators doing their job; sometimes glazing or debris. Grinding is more serious — metal-on-metal is a don't-wait situation.
- Grinding or clunking over bumps — possible suspension wear: bushings, links, struts. Common as vehicles age, especially on our roads.
- Clicking when turning — classic sign of a worn CV joint, but steering and suspension can mimic it.
- Car won't start — could be battery, alternator, starter, or connections. Rapid clicking often points at the battery side; dimming lights while driving more toward charging. {{CITY}} winters are hard on batteries — 4–5 years is a typical lifespan here.
- Check engine light — hundreds of possible causes, from a loose gas cap to sensors to emissions parts. Steady light: book a diagnostic soon. Flashing light: stop driving — that one can damage the engine.
- Overheating — possible thermostat, coolant leak, water pump, or fan issues. Don't keep driving an overheating car.
- Slipping or hard-shifting transmission — fluid condition, sometimes wear. Needs a proper look; not a guess-over-the-phone item.
- Vibration at speed — often wheel balance or tires; sometimes brakes (if felt when braking) or suspension.
- Pulling to one side — alignment, tire pressure/wear, or a dragging brake.
- Squealing on startup — often a belt or tensioner getting tired.
- Rust concerns — very common on older vehicles in Manitoba (road salt). Surface rust vs. structural rust are different conversations; an inspection tells which one it is.
- Fluid spots on the driveway — color helps (dark = oil, red/pink = transmission or power steering, green/orange = coolant), but source needs eyes on it.

## Make/model patterns (use gently, never as a verdict)
- Every make has known age/mileage patterns — it's fine to say "we do see that on those as they get up in mileage" when the caller's description matches a well-known pattern for their vehicle.
- Never trash-talk a caller's car and never promise their model is immune to a problem. If asked "is X reliable?", give a balanced one-liner: most vehicles are solid when maintained; some years/models have known weak spots, and an inspection tells the real story for their specific car.

## Maintenance by age and mileage (general guidance, not a quote)
- Oil changes: the single best habit — follow the interval for the vehicle; older engines often benefit from more frequent changes.
- Around 80,000–120,000 km: many vehicles need attention to brakes, tires, battery, and fluids; timing components on some engines.
- Seasonal (big deal in {{CITY}}): fall — battery test, block heater check, winter tires; spring — alignment and suspension check after pothole season, tire swap.
- Buying a used vehicle: a pre-purchase inspection is always worth it — that's exactly what the shop's inspections are for.

## Phrases that fit the shop's style
- "That does sound like something we sometimes see on those."
- "Could be a few different things — I wouldn't want to guess over the phone."
- "Best next step is to have our technicians take a proper look so we can pinpoint it."
- "It's worth checking sooner rather than later."
- For safety symptoms (brakes failing, wheel wobble, steering trouble, flashing check engine, overheating): "I'd rather you didn't drive it — let's arrange a tow, and I'll flag it urgent for {{OWNER_NAME}}."
